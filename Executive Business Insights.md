### 1. Executive Overview

The Week 6 analysis highlights opportunities for HealthConnect Rwanda to improve revenue collection, align staffing with patient demand, review emergency service utilization, and strengthen data quality.

The reference presentation reports RWF 9.31 billion in billed amounts, RWF 8.39 billion collected, and RWF 920 million outstanding across 11,887 bills. Emergency visits account for approximately one-third of the 5,000 appointment records.

However, incomplete links between appointment and billing records limit the reliability of some facility-level comparisons. Management should address these data limitations alongside operational improvements.

### 2. Key Business Insights

**Insight 1: Outstanding balances require focused collection follow-up.**

RWF 920 million remains outstanding across 11,887 bills, despite a reported collection rate of 90.1%.

**Business action:** Reconcile billing and payment records, prioritize bills with outstanding balances, and monitor collections and resolved balances weekly.

**Insight 2: Staffing should reflect differences in facility demand.**

HF013 recorded 278 appointments and HF014 recorded 276, compared with 217 at HF019. Reported staffing is relatively uniform across facilities.

**Business action:** Review shift-level workloads, waiting times, and staff allocation at the busiest facilities before making staffing changes.

**Insight 3: Emergency visits represent a significant share of activity.**

Approximately 1,650 of the 5,000 recorded appointments were emergency visits, representing 33%.

**Business action:** Analyze visit reasons, arrival patterns, and referral pathways to understand demand. The current figures alone do not establish whether emergency visits were avoidable.

**Insight 4: Chronic conditions deserve attention in care planning.**

Diabetes and hypertension each represent approximately 20% of recorded diagnoses. Together, they account for about 40% of diagnosis records.

**Business action:** Evaluate follow-up and continuity-of-care opportunities, while validating diagnosis coding and patient-level linkage.

**Insight 5: Some busy facilities also report lower patient ratings.**

HF013 and HF014 are among the busiest facilities, and both have an average reported rating of 2.77 out of 5.

**Business action:** Investigate waiting times, service processes, and patient feedback at these facilities before implementing targeted service improvements.

**Insight 6: Data quality affects the reliability of management decisions.**

The reference identifies missing facility details, incomplete appointment-to-bill links, and inconsistencies between billing status and recorded payments.

**Business action:** Improve record linkage, establish consistent identifiers, reconcile payment records, and document KPI definitions.

### 3. Priority Recommendations

1. **Improve collections:** Reconcile the outstanding-bill worklist and track recovery progress.
2. **Align staffing with demand:** Review workload and shift allocation at high-volume facilities.
3. **Investigate emergency demand:** Capture visit reasons and identify opportunities to improve patient pathways.
4. **Strengthen insurance claims monitoring:** Track approval rates, pending values, and claim-resolution times by insurer.
5. **Improve data completeness:** Maintain reliable facility reference data and consistent appointment, billing, and payment identifiers.
6. **Support chronic-care planning:** Use validated diagnosis information to assess follow-up needs for diabetes and hypertension.

### 4. Recommended 30-Day Action Plan

- **Week 1 — Finance and data teams:** Reconcile outstanding balances and payment-status inconsistencies.
- **Week 2 — Facility operations:** Review workload, waiting times, and patient ratings at HF013 and HF014.
- **Week 3 — Claims team:** Establish insurer-specific follow-up lists and monitor pending claims.
- **Week 4 — Data and analytics teams:** Improve record linkage, validate dashboard metrics, and document KPI definitions.

### 5. Data Limitations

The findings should be interpreted in light of the following limitations:

- The appointment file contains 5,000 records, while billing and diagnosis records extend to 60,000.
- Facility, department, and month analyses cover only a limited linked subset of bills.
- The reference does not include admissions, laboratory, prescription, or referral tables.
- Billing status can conflict with recorded payments.
- Diagnosis percentages describe recorded diagnoses and should not automatically be interpreted as unique-patient prevalence.
- Insurance identifiers alone do not confirm active coverage.

### 6. Executive Conclusion

HealthConnect Rwanda should prioritize outstanding-balance reconciliation, staffing alignment, emergency service analysis, and data-quality improvements. These initiatives offer practical opportunities to improve financial control and operational decision-making.

Before management relies on detailed facility comparisons, the organization should reconcile appointment, billing, and payment records. Better data completeness and consistent KPI definitions will strengthen future dashboards and support more reliable decisions.
